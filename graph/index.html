Largest known degree-4, diameter-4 graph

104 vertices and 208 edges. As of August 22nd, 2026, this is the largest known degree-4, diameter-4 graph. In simple terms, every vertex has 4 neighbors (degree-4), and no two vertices are more than 4 apart (diameter-4).

Building blocks

The graph is built from three K4,4 graphs: complete bipartite graphs between four "L" and four "R" vertices. Each Li and Rj is connected via a port Pij, for 24 vertices and 32 edges per K4,4.

The three of these account for 72 vertices and 96 edges. Every Li and Rj is already at degree 4; every port is at degree 2, short two edges.

Connecting the blocks

The remaining 32 vertices are connectors between the K4,4s, in 16 matched pairs: X00a/X00b through X15a/X15b. Each connector has one edge to its partner and one edge into each K4,4, bringing it to degree 4 and filling the 2 missing edges on each port.

Port assignment

This still leaves out the one thing that makes the graph work: the exact mapping between connectors and ports. That's the construction's free parameter, and it's what gets the diameter down to 4. One such assignment is shown below.

The whole thing

The full graph: three K4,4s down the left, 16 connector pairs on the right.

I originally found an asymmetric variant of this graph via a Z8 voltage graph lift of a 13-vertex base multigraph. After much effort yet failing to push past n=104, I revisited the graph and noticed it could be simplified into the more structured form shown here. I'm 40% confident it's optimal, but time will tell. :)

Given the progress I made here with the assistance of Claude Fable, I decided to let it loose on the rest of the degree-diameter table, with good success.
